HOW THIS COMPARISON WORKS

SAME BASELINE

Every modulation gets the same signal power, the same noise density, and the same jammer. Signal-to-noise is measured in a 16 kHz reference band. Digital modulations all send 8 kilobits per second.

WHAT IS SIMULATED

AM and FM are simulated as real waveforms: noise and the jammer are added, the signal is filtered to the receiver's bandwidth, and demodulated with an envelope detector (AM) or a frequency discriminator (FM). Digital modulations are simulated at the symbol level with ideal timing: coherent detection for BPSK and QPSK, and non-coherent detection for ASK and FSK.

JAMMER TYPES

Noise is wideband noise covering the signal. Tone on carrier is a steady tone at the carrier frequency. Tone +3 kHz is a steady tone 3 kHz above the carrier, inside the receiver passband of every modulation here. J/S is the jammer power over the signal power at the receiver.

LEFT OUT

No error correction, spread spectrum, hopping, fading, or directional antennas. Real systems use all of these and gain a lot of margin. The numbers show how the bare modulations compare, not what a fielded radio would do.

HOW IT WORKS AND WHAT IS LEFT OUT

CONSTELLATION

Each dot is one received symbol on the I (in-phase) and Q (quadrature) axes. White marks are where the transmitter meant the symbols to land. A symbol is decoded as the nearest white mark, so noise that pushes a dot across a gray boundary flips bits.

MORE BITS PER SYMBOL, LESS MARGIN

BPSK sends 1 bit per symbol, QPSK 2, 8-PSK 3, 16-QAM 4, 64-QAM 6. Packing more points into the same power puts them closer together, so less noise or jamming is needed to cause errors.

JAMMER TYPES

Noise adds random noise like a wideband jammer inside the link's bandwidth. Tone adds a single steady signal: it does nothing until it is strong enough to cross a decision boundary, and then it does a lot. Pulse keeps the same average power but delivers it in short bursts, so while a burst is on the jammer is much stronger than the signal.

JAMMER / SIGNAL (J/S)

The jammer's power divided by the signal's power at the receiver, in dB. 0 dB means equal. Jamming is only worth the effort when J/S is high enough to beat the link margin.

WHAT THIS LAB LEAVES OUT

Real links use error correction, spread spectrum, frequency hopping, interleaving, and directional antennas. All of those buy back margin against noise and jamming, and none are modeled here, so the numbers here are the worst case for an uncoded link. There is also no fading or multipath, and the receiver is assumed perfectly synchronized. BER curves for 8-PSK and the QAM modes use standard approximations.
