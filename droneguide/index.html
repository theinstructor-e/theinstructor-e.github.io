1 · What a Multirotor Is

You will be able to
  • Name the main parts of a small multirotor and what each does.
  • Place a drone in the DoD UAS group system.
  • Describe LiPo battery voltages and the basic care they need.

A small unmanned aircraft is a flying computer with four motors. To understand how it works, how to fly one, and how to counter one, you need to know its parts, because each part is a dependency.

The parts

PartJob
Frame and armsHold everything. A quadcopter has four arms, so four motors.
Motors and propellersBrushless motors spin the props to make thrust. The speed of each motor is controlled separately.
ESCsElectronic speed controllers. They take a signal from the flight controller and drive each motor.
Flight controller (FC)The brain. It reads gyroscopes and accelerometers many hundreds or thousands of times a second and adjusts the four motors to keep the drone stable and doing what you commanded.
BatteryUsually a lithium polymer (LiPo) pack. It is also the heaviest single part.
Radio receiverHears your control commands.
GPS and compassOptional. Give position, speed, and a heading reference for hold and return-to-home modes.
Camera and video transmitterSend what the drone sees back to the pilot, in FPV (first-person view) flying.

The four motors do not all spin the same way. Diagonal pairs spin one direction and the other pair spins the opposite way. Each spinning motor pushes back on the frame with a twisting force (torque). Two spinning one way and two spinning the other make those twists cancel, so the drone does not spin on its own.

Military UAS groups

The Department of Defense sorts unmanned aircraft into five groups by weight, altitude, and speed. If a UAS meets the limits of a higher group on any one measure, it goes in that higher group.

The group is set by the highest of the three. A light drone that flies fast or high still moves up a group.

LiPo batteries in brief

A LiPo pack is built from cells. Each cell is about 3.7 V nominal, about 4.2 V fully charged. Packs are named by how many cells are in series: 4S is four cells. So a 4S pack is 14.8 V nominal and 16.8 V full.

  • Do not discharge a cell below about 3.0 V, and land well before that. A common rule is to land by about 3.5 V per cell under load.
  • Charge only with the right charger, set to the right cell count, in a safe place and never unattended.
  • A swollen, punctured, or crushed pack is a fire hazard. Do not use it. Dispose of it through the proper channel.
Why a 17E caresEvery part on this list is something a counter-drone system can target. The control receiver, the video link, and the GPS are radio dependencies. The battery limits how long the drone can stay up. Knowing the parts is knowing the weak points.

2 · How It Flies

You will be able to
  • Explain how throttle, pitch, roll, and yaw come from four motor speeds.
  • Calculate thrust-to-weight ratio and estimate hover throttle.
  • Say why a tilted drone needs more thrust to hold height.

Four commands

A quadcopter has only four motors and can do six things (move in three directions, rotate about three axes) because it tilts. The pilot has four controls. On the standard Mode 2 transmitter:

StickControlWhat it does
Left, up and downThrottleTotal thrust: climb or descend.
Left, left and rightYawRotate left or right on the spot, nose swinging around.
Right, up and downPitchTilt nose down to go forward, nose up to go back.
Right, left and rightRollTilt to the side to slide left or right.

The mixer

The flight controller blends the four commands into four motor speeds. Throttle raises or lowers all four together. To pitch forward it speeds up the rear motors and slows the front ones, which tips the nose down. To roll right it speeds up the left side and slows the right. To yaw it speeds up one diagonal pair and slows the other, so the net twist on the frame turns the drone.

The drone is seen from above, nose up on the screen. Bars show motor speed. The arrow on each motor shows its spin direction. Move one slider at a time and watch which motors change.

Thrust and weight

To hover, total thrust must equal weight. The ratio of maximum thrust to weight is the thrust-to-weight ratio (TWR). A relaxed camera drone might have a TWR of about 2. A racing drone may be 8 or more. Because thrust rises roughly with throttle, hover throttle is about 1 ÷ TWR as a rough guide.

TWR = (number of motors × max thrust per motor) ÷ weight
hover throttle ≈ 100% ÷ TWR (rough: thrust is not exactly linear)
Try it: thrust-to-weight

Leaning costs thrust

To move sideways, the drone tilts so part of its thrust points horizontally. But only the vertical part holds it up. To keep the same height, total thrust must rise.

thrust needed = weight ÷ cos(tilt angle)
horizontal acceleration ≈ g × tan(tilt angle) g = 9.81 m/s²
Try it: lean and thrust
At steep angles the drone needs far more thrust to hold height. A drone with low thrust margin cannot lean as hard, which limits its top speed and how fast it can accelerate.
Why a 17E caresHow a drone moves tells you how it will react when you disturb it. Lose a motor or a link and it does not just stop: it responds the way its flight controller was set up to, and you can predict it from this chapter.
Check yourself
Summary
  • Four commands become four motor speeds through the mixer. Yaw uses the difference between the two diagonal pairs.
  • TWR = total max thrust ÷ weight. Hover throttle ≈ 100% ÷ TWR.
  • Thrust needed = weight ÷ cos(tilt). Acceleration ≈ g × tan(tilt).

3 · Flight Modes and Failsafe

You will be able to
  • Describe acro, angle, altitude hold, and position hold modes.
  • Explain what failsafe does when the control link is lost.
  • Predict what a drone does when each dependency is removed.

How much help does the flight controller give?

A flight mode decides how much the flight controller does for the pilot.

Try it: flight modes

Failsafe

The receiver tells the flight controller whether it is still getting commands. If the link is lost, after a short timeout the controller runs a failsafe: a pre-set action. Common ones are:

  • Disarm / drop. Motors stop. Common on racing drones with no GPS.
  • Land. Descend in place at a gentle rate.
  • Hover. Hold position, if the drone knows where it is.
  • Return to home (RTH). Climb to a set height and fly back to where it took off. It needs a GPS fix and a recorded home point.

Which one applies is a setting, and it depends on what the drone has. A drone with no GPS cannot return home.

Try it: the link drops
General behaviors for teaching. Real drones vary by model and setup.

Wind and GPS

A drone flying in wind has an airspeed and a ground speed that differ. It may fly steadily relative to the air yet move quickly over the ground, and a small low-power drone may not be able to make headway against strong wind. If GPS is lost, a drone that relied on it for position hold will drift with the wind in an attitude-only mode, and the pilot has to fly it by hand.

Why a 17E caresFailsafe is the reason “jam the control link” does not always mean “the drone falls.” The outcome is whatever the owner programmed. Knowing the options tells you what the drone will probably do next.
Check yourself
Summary
  • Acro gives no help. Angle self-levels. Altitude hold and position hold need a barometer and GPS.
  • Failsafe is a pre-set action when the link is lost: drop, land, hover, or return home.
  • RTH needs a GPS fix and a home point. A drone with no GPS cannot do it.

4 · Power and Endurance

You will be able to
  • Calculate battery energy from cells and capacity.
  • Estimate flight time from average current.
  • Explain why endurance is short for small drones.

A hovering multirotor is constantly pushing air down to stay up, so it uses power all the time. Endurance is limited by how much energy the battery holds.

pack voltage (nominal) = cells × 3.7 V
energy (watt-hours) = voltage × capacity in amp-hours
flight time (minutes) ≈ capacity (Ah) × usable fraction ÷ average current (A) × 60

Use about 80% as the usable fraction: you land before the pack is flat. Batteries are rated in mAh (1,000 mAh = 1 Ah).

Worked example: 4S 1500 mAh at 20 A average

Voltage = 4 × 3.7 = 14.8 V. Energy = 14.8 × 1.5 Ah = 22.2 Wh.

Usable capacity = 1.5 × 0.8 = 1.2 Ah. Flight time = 1.2 ÷ 20 × 60 = 3.6 minutes.

Average power = 14.8 V × 20 A = 296 W. Racing drones fly hard and burn through a pack in a few minutes. A drone built for efficiency, with large slow props and a lighter load, averages much less current and flies for a long time.

Try it: endurance
Bigger batteries add energy but also weight, which raises current. The relationship is not linear, so doubling the battery does not double the time. Land by the voltage shown. Voltage sags under load, so watch it while flying, not just on the ground.

What that means in the field

  • Small drones are short-lived. Typical small multirotors fly for minutes to a few tens of minutes. The enemy and friendly pilots alike have to land and swap batteries.
  • Cold hurts batteries. LiPo packs lose capacity and voltage in cold weather. Keep them warm until use.
  • Range is speed times time, and also depends on the link (Chapter 5). A drone that can fly 20 minutes at 15 m/s could cover 18 km, but only if the link and the navigation hold up the whole way.
Why a 17E caresBattery life is a clock on every drone mission. It limits loiter time, forces return trips, and tells you when to expect a drone to leave or land. Operators who must conserve battery also tend to fly in predictable patterns.
Check yourself
Summary
  • Energy (Wh) = cells × 3.7 V × capacity (Ah).
  • Flight time ≈ capacity × 0.8 ÷ average current × 60 minutes.
  • Endurance is short because hovering burns power constantly and batteries are heavy.

5 · Radio and Video Links

You will be able to
  • Name the common drone control and video frequency bands.
  • Estimate link range from power, antenna gains, and sensitivity.
  • Explain polarization and the radio horizon for a drone link.

A piloted drone has two radio links. One carries commands up, and the other carries video down. Both are ordinary radio, so everything from the Radio Waves and Modulation guides applies.

LinkCommon bandsNotes
Control2.4 GHz (very common), 900 MHz (long range), 5.8 GHz on some systemsOften frequency hopping, with a low data rate. Lower frequency goes farther and penetrates better.
Video5.8 GHz (analog FPV and some digital systems), 2.4 GHz on someWide bandwidth, so it needs a strong signal. Analog degrades gradually with noise. Digital often holds then drops.
GPS1.2 and 1.5 GHz, receive onlyFaint, see GNSS & Navigation Explained.

Range comes from the link budget you already know: transmit power plus both antenna gains, minus path loss, compared with the receiver’s sensitivity, with margin for fading.

allowed path loss = Pt + Gt + Gr − receiver sensitivity − fade margin
free-space range (km) = 10(allowed path loss − 32.44 − 20·log10(f in MHz)) ÷ 20

The free-space figure is a ceiling. In practice the radio horizon or obstacles cut it short. The horizon limit is 4.12 × (√h1 + √h2) km with heights in metres, as in Tactical Radio Basics.

Try it: how far will it link?
Receiver sensitivities and powers are illustrative teaching values for each link type, not figures for any product. Real range is usually less than the smaller of the two numbers, because of trees, buildings, interference, and antenna pointing.

Polarization

FPV video antennas are usually circularly polarized, in one of two directions, right-hand (RHCP) or left-hand (LHCP). Circular polarization resists the fading caused by signals bouncing off the ground and buildings. But the transmit and receive antennas must have the same hand. Opposite hands lose a large amount of signal, often 20 dB or more. If video is noisy at short range, check the antennas match.

Why a 17E caresThese numbers tell you how much power an enemy needs to take a link away, and from how far. A control link at a few kilometres is one that a modest jammer or a sensitive detector can reach. See the Modulation & Noise guide for what a jammer does to a link.
Check yourself
Summary
  • Control links use 2.4 GHz or 900 MHz. Video is often 5.8 GHz.
  • Allowed path loss = Pt + Gt + Gr − sensitivity − margin. Free-space range follows from it, and the horizon caps it.
  • Circular polarization needs matching hands. Opposite hands lose 20 dB or more.

Rules you need to know

In the United States the FAA regulates drone flying. These are the headline rules for small aircraft. Always check the current rules, and follow your unit and range control. Military installations, airfields, and restricted areas have their own limits.

Two lessons. First, there is no single answer: “jam the control link” defeats a manually flown drone but not a drone on a pre-programmed route. Second, a drone with no radio link at all, such as one that is controlled by a thin optical fibre that unspools behind it, is untouched by RF jamming and gives a passive RF detector nothing to hear. That is why defence needs layers: radio-frequency tools, radar, optical and acoustic sensors, and kinetic options.

Why a 17E caresYou will be asked what will work against a particular drone. Start from its dependencies: what does it need to fly, to see, and to know where it is? The answer points to the effect that will work and the ones that will not.
