Georg Ohm, 1827 — made electricity mathematical
◆ Lesson Alignment — B10 LSA 1-3
Voltage is electrical "pressure" (like water pressure in a tank), current is the flow of charge (like water through a pipe), and resistance opposes that flow (like narrowing the pipe). Ohm's Law links all three. For a conductor under constant conditions, raising voltage raises current, while raising resistance reduces it.
Water Analogy & Ohm's Law Triangle
VOLTAGE = Pressure
Higher water level → more pressure → pushes harder. Measured in Volts.
CURRENT = Flow rate
Amount of water moving past a point per second. Measured in Amps.
RESISTANCE = Pipe width
A narrower pipe resists flow. Measured in Ohms (Ω).
POWER = Work done
Pressure × flow = how much work the water can do. Watts.
1 Amp = 6.28 × 10¹⁸ electrons passing a point per second. The symbol "I" comes from the French intensité du courant — "intensity of the current."

◆ Lesson Alignment — B10 LSA 4
DC flows in one constant direction (batteries, solar, fuel cells) — steady voltage, ideal for electronics, but it can't be transformed up/down and loses power over distance (P_loss = I²R). AC reverses direction periodically (from an alternator), is easily stepped up/down by transformers, and travels long distances efficiently — which is why it won the "War of the Currents" for the grid.

◆ Lesson Alignment — B10 LSA 5
Resistors limit current and set operating points; capacitors store energy in an electric field and pass AC while blocking DC; inductors store energy in a magnetic field and oppose changes in current. Together (LC networks) they form the resonant circuits, filters, and antenna-matching networks at the heart of every radio, jammer, and receiver.

Voltage & Current Phase
AMBER = Voltage (E)  |  CYAN = Current (I)
ELI the ICE man: In an Inductor (L), voltage (E) leads current (I) → E-L-I. In a Capacitor (C), current (I) leads voltage (E) → I-C-E. Resistive loads are in-phase.
◆ Lesson Alignment — B10 LSA 6 (Power Factor)
Power Factor = real power (useful work, watts) ÷ apparent power (total supplied, VA) — the "beer mug" model: beer is real power, foam is reactive power, the full mug is apparent power. In-phase = PF 1.0 = max power transfer = efficient antenna/jammer coupling. Out of phase = power reflects = overheating, reduced jamming range, high SWR. A poor PF on a generator wastes fuel and raises your acoustic/IR/RF signature.

◆ Lesson Alignment — B11 Impedance & VSWR
Impedance (Z) is total opposition to AC/RF — resistance (turns energy to heat) plus reactance (capacitors/inductors storing and returning energy). When the load doesn't match the line, energy reflects back, forming standing waves measured by VSWR. 1:1 is perfect; above 3:1 risks damaging the transmitter's power amplifier from reflected heat. Fix it by matching components, tuning the antenna, and minimizing cable/connectors.
