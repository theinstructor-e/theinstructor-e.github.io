▸ DF FUNDAMENTALS REFERENCE▶
Propagation & DF Range
This trainer simulates line-of-sight (LOS) tactical DF — the standard mode for VHF/UHF man-portable and vehicle-mounted systems. LOS range is limited by the radio horizon and by terrain blocking the path.

For reference: ground wave (LF/MF) follows the earth's curvature well beyond LOS but attenuates fast over land. Sky wave (HF) reflects off the ionosphere for very long range, but ionospheric variability degrades bearing accuracy and isn't modeled at this map's tactical scale.
Radio Horizon
A taller antenna sees farther before the earth's curvature blocks the path.
Horizon (km) ≈ 4.12 × √(height in meters)
A 2m handheld sees only ~6 km — a small fraction of this 30 km map. That's by design: a handheld is genuinely short-range. Beyond that range, the sensor shows "DETECTED" with a wavering line instead of a solid bearing — you know something's out there, but the signal is too weak to resolve a reliable direction. That's a distance problem, not a bug — move the sensor closer to the emitter, or switch to Vehicle/Tower.
Terrain Masking
LOS DF needs a clear path — hills and ridgelines block VHF/UHF signals entirely, unlike HF sky wave which can go over them. A sensor flagged "TERRAIN MASKED" has its path physically blocked, separate from horizon distance. Either way, a masked sensor contributes nothing usable to the fix — reposition to restore line of sight, or get within range.
Antenna Height vs. Mobility
Taller antennas extend your horizon and tighten bearing accuracy (less ground-level multipath/clutter) — but a 30m OP mast takes time to erect and isn't something you displace quickly. A 2m handheld sets up instantly but sees less and has noisier bearings. Site selection is a real tradeoff, not just "taller is better."
Baseline Geometry
Even with perfect bearings, sensors bunched close together create shallow crossing angles and a large CEP. Spreading sensors out (a wider baseline) sharpens the crossing angle and shrinks the fix — see the Concave/Convex demos.
Biomes — Same Physics, Different Environment
Mountainous uses varied terrain elevation — a taller antenna can clear a lower hill but not a taller one (each hill lists its peak height). Desert is flat and dry, extending range and sharpening bearings versus baseline. Urban buildings block LOS regardless of antenna height (dense structures beat even an elevated OP) and cause heavy multipath — the hardest environment here. Forest canopy and tree trunks attenuate signal and add moderate multipath — worse than open terrain, though not as severe as Urban.

▶ HOW TO USE
Use the "Tap Map To Place" buttons above the map to choose whether tapping places a Sensor or the Emitter — works on both touchscreens and desktop. Pick an antenna type first — it changes your radio horizon and bearing accuracy. (On desktop, right-click always places the emitter directly, regardless of mode.)

With Terrain Realism on, hills can mask a sensor's line of bearing entirely — that sensor won't contribute to the fix until you reposition it.
