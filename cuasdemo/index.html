◆ Core Idea
A small UAS is only as good as its radio links. It needs a control uplink to take commands, often a video downlink to see, and GNSS to know where it is. Every one of those is an RF signal — and every RF signal can be detected, jammed, or spoofed. Counter-UAS EW attacks these dependencies rather than the airframe itself.

◆ Teaching Matrix
Try each combination: Jam C2 + RTH sends it home; + Hover freezes it; + Land drops it in place; + Continue shows a GPS-guided drone ignoring the C2 jam entirely — proving you must also deny GNSS. Jam GNSS strips navigation so it drifts and can't hold target. Spoof GNSS feeds false position and walks the drone to your capture point. Same jammer, very different results.

DECIDE is the hinge: identification and rules of engagement come before defeat. Jamming or firing on an unidentified track can hit a friendly drone, a hobbyist, or violate airspace/authorities. Positive ID and clearance separate a clean engagement from an incident.
◆ Why a Kill Chain
C-UAS isn't one action, it's a sequence, and it can break at any link. You can't defeat what you can't identify; you can't identify what you can't track; you can't track what you never detected. Layered sensors feed the early steps; EW and other effectors serve the Defeat step; and Assess tells you whether to re-engage. A gap anywhere lets the threat through.
