Tell the Schoolhouse How Our Graduates Are Doing

You see the Soldiers we send you every day. Five minutes of your honest feedback tells us what to keep, what to fix, and what to add.

What we are asking

This is for NCOs who supervise or work with 17E Soldiers fresh out of AIT. Short answers are fine. Specifics help the most.

1 · Keep doing

What they know that is good

Skills and habits your new Soldiers arrive with that make your job easier.

2 · Fix or add

What they do not know but you wish they did

Gaps you had to teach on arrival, or equipment and tasks we do not cover.

3 · Reach back

Stay in touch

Ask for a call, a question answered, or an instructor visit to your unit.

The feedback form

Unclassified only. Do not enter classified information, CUI, personal details about a Soldier (names, SSNs, medical, or counseling information), or operational details of your unit. Describe skills and tasks in general terms.
Open the form in a new tab

If the form will not open on your network, try it on a personal device or email us instead (below).

Preview the questions (about 5 minutes)
  • About you. Unit, your role, how long you have worked with 17E Soldiers, how many you have received in the past year. Name and email are optional.
  • What they do well. Rate new arrivals from 1 to 5 on topics such as basic electronics, radio and RF fundamentals, networking, EW concepts, SDR and other tools, troubleshooting, Army basics, navigation, reports, and initiative. Then tell us in your own words what they do best.
  • What they are missing. Pick the top three things you wish they knew on arrival, and tell us what you had to teach them first.
  • Your equipment. Systems or tasks your unit uses that we should be teaching.
  • Reach back. Whether we may contact you, whether you want a call or visit, and any other comments.

Reach back

Prefer to talk or write? Questions about a Soldier’s training, a topic you want covered, or a visit to your unit are all welcome.

What happens to your feedback

Instructors read the responses and use them to adjust what we teach and how. Comments are summarized by theme, so no one is quoted by name without asking first.
