HOW IT WORKS AND WHAT IS LEFT OUT

EMCON

Emission control is the planned, selective use of your emitters to keep command and control working while making you harder to detect. It is a trade, and the commander decides where to set it.

HOW THE ENEMY FINDS YOU

Each enemy station listens for your transmissions. If a signal arrives above its detection limit, it takes a bearing. One station gives a line of bearing. Two stations give a fix where the lines cross. The more intercepts, the tighter the fix. Once the fix is tight enough, you can be engaged.

WHAT SETS YOUR SIGNATURE

Power and distance decide whether a signal reaches the sensor. Time on the air decides how many chances the enemy gets. Terrain between you and a sensor can block it. Frequency matters: HF and VHF carry far, while 2.4 and 5.8 GHz signals only reach a few kilometers and are blocked easily. Direction matters: a satellite terminal points up, so little of its power reaches a ground sensor.

YOUR OPTIONS

Off removes the emitter. Scheduled means a few short, planned transmissions. Restricted means lower power and brief, disciplined transmissions. Normal is unrestricted use. Displacing moves the command post so the enemy has to start over, but you lose communications while you move.

LEFT OUT

This is a training abstraction. It uses simplified free-space propagation with a flat penalty for a ridge, two fixed enemy sensors, and generic emitter numbers. It does not model real equipment, real detection ranges, or real direction-finding accuracy, and it leaves out jamming, decoys, deception, and enemy sensors in the air and in space. The four posture settings are a teaching simplification, not doctrinal EMCON levels.
