Simple Tech

Hide Site
Watching without being seen.

How a small recon or sniper team picks, occupies, and lays out a position built for observation, not defense.

Hide site — built to observe, not to fight

A hide site is a naturally covered and concealed position, away from a unit's primary positions, that protects a small team while letting it use small arms and sensors to observe a target area.

🔭 Think of it like a duck blind, not a foxhole: a patrol base is built to rest and defend a whole squad. A hide site is built for two to four people to disappear completely while they watch something specific — the goal is to never be found, not to win a fight if you are.

How it differs from a patrol base

Patrol BaseHide Site
Team sizeSquad or platoonTypically 2–4 (recon or sniper team)

If discoveredDefend or break contact as a unitEvacuate — the position is not meant to be defended
🔗Same family as the ORP and patrol base. Like those, the leader selects a primary and an alternate site before the team ever moves — for both the hide position and the surveillance position.

Site selection — BUES

Before committing to a site, the team checks it against four criteria remembered as BUES.

BBlend in with the surrounding area — does the site look like everything around it, with no reason to stand out?UUnexpected sites should be used — avoid the obvious "good" spot, since the enemy sees the same terrain you do.EEvacuation routes are planned during site selection — know the link-up location with friendly forces before you need it.SAvoid silhouetting the site — use the sides of terrain features rather than the crest or a hard skyline.
🎯 The obvious spot is the trap. A hilltop with a perfect view is perfect for the enemy to check too. BUES exists because the position that looks ideal to you often looks exactly as ideal to whoever is looking for you.

Primary and alternate, for both positions

The leader selects tentative sites during planning — by physical reconnaissance, aerial observation, photographs, or map recon — and picks primary and alternate hide sites as well as primary and alternate surveillance sites. Before the team commits, the leader confirms the site with a physical reconnaissance and can still move it if it turns out to be unsuitable.

🌦️Don't forget sustainment. Along with observation and fields of fire, radio, and cover/concealment, the site has to support the team for the planned duration — food, water, waste, and weather all factor into which site actually works.

Occupation

How the team actually moves into the site matters as much as where the site is — the wrong approach can lead the enemy straight to it.

🪝
Fishhook / Dog-Leg Method
Standard approach

The team continues past the site on its original line of march, then turns sharply back into it — the same trail-breaking logic as a patrol base's 90° entry, just executed as a hook or dog-leg rather than a single turn.

⚡
Occupation by Force
Last resort

Used when time is a major limiting factor. The leader still conducts a reconnaissance, but the team moves directly into the tentative site rather than working a hook or dog-leg.

Leader selects tentative sites
During planning, using map recon, imagery, or prior knowledge of the area.
Leader conducts a physical reconnaissance
Confirms the site works before the team commits — and can relocate it if it doesn't.
Team occupies using Fishhook/Dog-Leg or Occupation by Force
Team establishes the hide position, then the separate surveillance position

Layout — two positions, not one

The single biggest layout difference from a patrol base: the hide position and the observation/surveillance position are kept apart, connected by a covered route.

🛌
Hide Position
Rest, admin, security

Where the team not actively observing rests, maintains equipment, and maintains security — fully concealed, not necessarily with a view of the target.

👁️
Surveillance / OP Position
Observation & fields of fire

Forward of the hide position, chosen for observation and fields of fire toward the named area of interest (NAI) or target — occupied only as long as needed, by only as many people as needed.

Keeping them separate means that if the observation position is compromised, the rest of the team and its equipment at the hide position are not automatically exposed too — and vice versa.

🚪Evacuation route. Per BUES, this is planned before occupation, not improvised afterward — the team should already know how it leaves and where it links up if the site is compromised.
