Grid coordinates — 6-digit practice on a 4 km map
Grid squares are 1,000 m. A 6-digit grid has three easting digits then three northing digits: read right, then up, with each digit group ending in tenths of a square (100 m).
Pick a mode above, then answer.
REMEMBER: easting first (right), northing second (up). The grid you give names the 100 m square whose lower-left corner is at those numbers. With the guides off, use the tick marks on the map edge. On a phone, turn it sideways for a bigger map.

Practice — work the leg by hand
You get two points. Work out the grid azimuth and the distance with a protractor or with trigonometry, then check.
Enter your answers and press Check. Azimuth within 2° and distance within 50 m counts as correct.
BY HAND: east difference (ΔE) and north difference (ΔN) between the points. The azimuth is the angle whose tangent is ΔE ÷ ΔN, adjusted for the quadrant. The distance is the square root of (ΔE² + ΔN²).
