FOOM — how a unit moves

You will be able to
  • Say what FOOM stands for and the two decisions a leader makes.
  • Name the four trade-offs and say why you cannot maximize all of them.

FOOM stands for Formations and Order of Movement. Before a squad or platoon moves, its leader makes two choices: how the unit is arranged (the formation) and how carefully it moves (the movement technique).

🚗 Like a convoy of cars: tight and fast on an open highway, spread out and cautious in fog. The road and the risk decide, not habit.

The two decisions

🔺
Formation
How you are arranged

Where each Soldier, team, or squad sits relative to the others. It sets your security, firepower, and how easy the unit is to control.

🚶
Movement technique
How carefully you move

Traveling, traveling overwatch, or bounding overwatch. It sets your speed and how ready you are if contact happens.

🧭How leaders decide. The infantry platoon and squad publication (ATP 3-21.8) says leaders choose a movement technique based on the likelihood of enemy contact and the requirement for speed. They weigh formations against their METT-TC(I) analysis.

Every choice is a trade-off

SpeedHow fast you can cover ground.SecurityHow well you can see and protect in every direction.ControlHow easily the leader can see, signal, and direct the unit.FirepowerHow much fire you can put on a target, and in which direction.

You cannot maximize all four. The fastest, easiest-to-control formations tend to be the least secure. The most secure techniques are the slowest. A good leader picks the mix the mission and the enemy call for right now, and changes it when things change.

Formations

You will be able to
  • Match each fire team and squad formation to when it is best used.
  • Say why a file is the least secure and when a line is used.

A formation is a shape. Each one gives you more of some things and less of others, and each one has a place.

Fire team and squad formations

LevelFormationBest forWatch for
Fire teamWedgeThe fire team’s basic formation. It expands and contracts to fit the terrain, with good security in all directions.In rough terrain or poor visibility, reduce intervals so everyone can still see the team leader.
Fire teamFileSpeed and easy control in restrictive terrain, like dense woods or a ridge line.The least secure. Weak flank and rear security, so avoid it when you expect contact.
SquadColumn (fire teams in wedge)The squad’s main formation for movement, unless it is preparing to assault. Good spread across and in depth without losing control.When terrain or visibility makes control hard, the wedge is modified into a file (a modified column).
SquadLineMaximum firepower to the front. Used to assault, or just before it.Poor flank security and hard to control while moving.
SquadFileTight terrain and fast movement.Same weaknesses as any file. Normally used only for traveling.
🔧Formations bend. Leaders adjust intervals and positions to fit the ground. As the terrain eases and control gets easier, Soldiers go back to their original positions.

Platoon formations

At platoon level the publication lists column, line, vee, wedge, and file. Two examples of how they work:

ColumnThe lead squad is the base squad, and the other squads follow it.WedgeThe lead squad is the base squad, with the other two behind, overwatching or trailing. It works with traveling and traveling overwatch and lets the platoon change quickly to bounding overwatch.
⚠️Check your class slides. The WTBD tool and some courses also show echelon and diamond. The current infantry platoon and squad publication does not list them as platoon formations. Use whatever your course teaches for testing, and ask your instructor how it fits.

When to change formations

Move in your main formation, and go to another one when the situation calls for it. A squad column in wedge is the everyday choice. When the terrain squeezes the unit, it goes to a modified column. When it is ready to assault, it goes to a line.

Movement techniques

You will be able to
  • Match each movement technique to its use, speed, and security.
  • Tell alternate bounds from successive bounds.

The movement technique is about how ready you are for contact. Pick it by how likely contact is, and how much speed you need.

TechniqueUse it whenSpeedSecurityHow it works
TravelingSpeed is needed and contact is not likelyFastestLeastThe unit moves together at a steady pace.
Traveling overwatchContact is possibleModerateModerateThe lead element keeps moving. The trailing element follows farther back, ready to support by fire.
Bounding overwatchContact is expected, the leader feels the enemy is near, or a large open danger area must be crossedSlowestMostOne element moves while another covers it from a good position. Only one element moves at a time.
📏In traveling overwatch, the squad normally moves in column or wedge, with attached weapons near and under the squad leader’s control. Ideally the lead team moves at least 50 meters ahead of the rest.

Bounding overwatch, in detail

The overwatch element covers the moving element from covered, concealed positions, with good observation and fields of fire against likely enemy positions. It scans assigned sectors. The bounding element uses cover and concealment, must not block the overwatch element’s fires, and must never move farther than the overwatch element can support. The squad leader usually stays with the overwatch team.

MethodHow it movesTrade-off
Alternate boundsThe rear element moves past the lead element and takes up overwatch, then they switch again.Usually faster.
Successive boundsThe lead element advances while the rear covers. The rear element then moves up to about even with it. The lead element moves again.Easier to control, but slower.

Signs that contact is close

Leaders shift to bounding overwatch when signs suggest the enemy is near: movement, noise, reflections, trash, fresh tracks, or even a hunch. Trust the feeling and slow down.

🔄Techniques change during a movement. A patrol may travel in the open, use traveling overwatch as it nears a tree line, and bound across a danger area. Choose again whenever the chance of contact changes.

Control and order of movement

You will be able to
  • Say where leaders, weapons, and security go.
  • List ways to stay in control of a moving unit.

The order of movement is who goes where inside the formation. Get it right, and the leader can control the unit and everyone knows which way to watch.

Where things go

LeadersPlace leaders where they can see the unit and be seen, and where they can direct it. In bounding overwatch the squad leader usually stays with the overwatch team.WeaponsKeep key weapons near the leader so they can be used quickly. In traveling overwatch, attached weapons move near and under the squad leader’s control.SecuritySomeone watches every direction. The platoon leader is responsible for 360-degree security and for making sure subordinate units’ sectors of fire support each other.

Staying in control

Watch your intervals
Tighten them in thick terrain, at night, or in poor visibility so Soldiers can see their leader. Open them up when you can, for dispersion.
Know your signals
Hand-and-arm signals and the radio keep the unit together without noise. Bounding especially needs clear communication so the two elements never confuse who is moving.
Have a base element
In a formation, one element sets the pace. If it halts, the formation halts.
Be ready to change
A wedge can shift quickly to bounding overwatch. A column can shift to a line before an assault. Rehearse the transitions.
🗺️Route planning matters. Planning and selecting a route is a key leader skill. Look at the ground before you move, and mark the danger areas where you expect to change techniques.
🔗Connect it to planning. In troop leading procedures, formation and technique come out of your METT-TC analysis in step 3 and are checked during rehearsals in step 8. See TLP Explained.

Worked example — one patrol, three techniques

This is an illustration. Real patrols make these calls by reading the ground and the threat.

🧭 The mission: a squad moves 3 km from a secure assembly area to an observation point overlooking a road. Enemy activity has been reported in the area.
LegSituationTechniqueFormationWhy
1. Out of the assembly areaInside friendly lines, on a wide track. Contact not likely.TravelingSquad column, fire teams in wedgeSpeed matters and the risk is low. The column is the everyday movement formation.
2. Toward the tree lineThick woods ahead. Enemy reported nearby but not seen. Contact possible.Traveling overwatchSquad column, modified toward a fileThe lead team moves ahead, ready for contact. Dense woods make control hard, so the wedge tightens and files.
3. Crossing an open fieldA large open area with hills beyond it. Nobody knows what is in the far tree line.Bounding overwatchSquad column, fire teams in wedgeA large open danger area is exactly when to bound. One team overwatches while the other crosses.
4. Reaching the observation pointContact is expected at the objective.Bounding overwatchSquad column, ready to go on lineThe squad slows down, and keeps its options open if it has to assault.
💡What to notice. The technique and the formation are separate choices. The leader read the ground and the risk on every leg, and changed one, the other, or both.
