◆ Lesson Alignment — B14 / B15 Wave Propagation
FSPL follows the inverse-square law from B14: power spreads over an expanding sphere, so every doubling of distance loses 6 dB. Doubling frequency also costs 6 dB because a fixed antenna's effective aperture shrinks. The exponent n captures real-world losses beyond ideal free space.

KEY: Path loss is the biggest factor in any link budget. The 6 dB rule (double distance = +6 dB loss) lets you estimate range tradeoffs in your head — critical for predicting both your comms range and an adversary's intercept range.

◆ Lesson Alignment — B15 Signals & Waves
Antenna gain does NOT add power — it focuses the same power into a narrower beam, like a flashlight reflector. This is the directivity concept from B15. Higher gain trades coverage angle for range in the pointed direction.

KEY: Gain and beamwidth are linked — you can't have both wide coverage and high gain. Directional antennas give range and reduce intercept footprint; omni antennas cover all directions but radiate to adversary collectors too.
