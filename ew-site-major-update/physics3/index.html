◆ Lesson Alignment — B18 (LSA 1-3)
In the linear region, output is directly proportional to input — radios transmit clean, receivers amplify accurately, EW systems behave predictably. Pushed into the non-linear region (overdriven, poorly matched, beyond design limits) you get distortion, saturation, unwanted harmonics, and splatter. Harmonics occur at integer multiples of the fundamental; sub-harmonics at fractions.
Spectrum — Fundamental & Harmonics
Harmonics appear at regular spacing, decreasing in amplitude
WHY IT MATTERS TO EW: If you don't understand harmonics you might think there are multiple enemy transmitters when there's only one, sense the wrong frequency at lower power, or jam a harmonic and waste power — giving away your position. Verify: regularly-spaced peaks + amplitude drop = harmonics of one source.
FIELD FIX (if you emit harmonics): reduce power/input, use a better-matched load/antenna, insert a band-pass or low-pass filter, check connections/cables, and keep equipment cool — thermal stress pushes devices non-linear.

◆ Lesson Alignment — B19 (LSA 1-4)
Path loss is signal weakening as the wavefront spreads spherically — the same energy spread over a larger area as distance grows. As frequency rises, wavelength shrinks and antennas collect less, so loss increases. Line-of-sight losses include absorption, diffraction, scattering, free-space, ground-reflection, and polarization mismatch. Link margin is how much signal you have above the receiver's sensitivity floor — positive margin = working link.
FRESNEL ZONE: the elliptical region around the direct path that must stay clear of obstacles. Blocking even part of it adds loss even with visual line-of-sight. The ionosphere's D-layer absorbs LF; E/F layers refract MF-HF; the F-layer carries long-range skywave.

◆ Lesson Alignment — B20 (LSA 1-2)
A signal carries information; noise is unwanted interference that degrades it (thermal, environmental, jamming). SNR is simply signal minus noise floor in dB. Barrage jamming raises the noise floor across many frequencies at once to bury the signal. Receiver sensitivity — the weakest detectable signal — sets your effective SNR floor.

Fixes: ferrite beads/chokes, shielding, filtering, grounding
FERRITE BEADS: act as frequency-dependent resistors — near-transparent at DC/low frequency, but high impedance at RF, absorbing noise currents as heat. Shielded cables act as a Faraday cage to block radiated EMI.
◆ Lesson Alignment — B21 EMI & Solar Effects
EMI is any unwanted EM energy that disrupts a system, coupling four ways: conductive (through wires/shared grounds — vehicle/generator power), inductive (magnetic fields from motors/alternators), capacitive (electric fields between parallel cables — crosstalk), and radiated (through the air from jammers/radar/antennas). Sources include switching supplies, ESD, lightning, poor PCB layout, harmonics, jamming — and operator error. Solar activity adds ionospheric disturbance.
