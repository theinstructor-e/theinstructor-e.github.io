Whatever you do to one side of an equation, do to the other. Isolate x by undoing operations in reverse order (undo addition/subtraction first, then multiplication/division).
◆ Why This Matters
Rearranging formulas is the single most-used algebra skill in EW work — solving Ohm's Law for R, solving a dB formula for a ratio, or solving a link-budget equation for required transmit power all boil down to isolating one variable.

◆ Where This Shows Up in EW
Antenna elevation angle vs. range, direction-finding bearing triangulation, and signal phase all reduce to right-triangle relationships. If you can find the angle and one side, trig gets you the rest without measuring in the field.
Quick check: sin and cos are always between −1 and 1. If your calculator gives you something outside that range for sin/cos, you made an input error — tan has no such limit.

Why Logs Matter
◆ Compressing Huge Ranges
A radio receiver might handle signal power from about 1 picowatt up to several watts — a range spanning 12+ orders of magnitude. A logarithmic scale compresses that entire range into a manageable set of numbers, which is exactly why decibels exist.
Key idea: a logarithm answers "what power do I raise the base to, to get this number?" log₁₀(1000) = 3 because 10³ = 1000.
