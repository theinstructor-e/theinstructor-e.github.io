Shutting the middle down

Encryption in transit (TLS/HTTPS)

If the payload is ciphertext, a relay can copy it but can't read or forge it. The lock icon means the tunnel is sealed end to end.

Certificate validation

The client checks that the server's certificate is signed by a trusted CA and matches the name. A swapped cert throws a warning — don't click past it.

Certificate pinning

The app hard-codes the exact cert or key it expects. Even a "valid" attacker cert is rejected outright.

HSTS + preload

Strict-Transport-Security tells the browser to only ever use HTTPS, killing the SSL-strip downgrade. Preloaded domains are protected even on the very first visit.

Mutual authentication

Both ends prove identity (client certs, signed tokens). The attacker can't impersonate a party it can't authenticate as.

Trusted networks + VPN

Avoid rogue Wi-Fi and unencrypted hotspots. A VPN wraps the whole session so the local network sees only ciphertext.
